Completing this IoT Summer School after my first year of Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) was a valuable learning experience. Before joining the program, I had a basic understanding of microcontrollers and embedded systems. Through hands-on assignments and practical simulations, I learned how different hardware and software components work together to build real-world IoT applications.

During the course, I worked with ESP32 and Arduino platforms while building projects using sensors such as the DHT11, ultrasonic sensor, LDR, PIR motion sensor, gas sensor, and soil moisture sensor. I also explored communication technologies like MQTT, HTTP requests, Bluetooth, and REST APIs by integrating services such as OpenWeatherMap. These activities helped me understand how sensor data is collected, processed, transmitted, and used to create smart connected systems.

Apart from programming and circuit design, I learned to use Wokwi for simulation, organize projects on GitHub, and write proper README documentation. I also gained a basic understanding of networking concepts like Wi-Fi security, MQTT Quality of Service (QoS), LoRa, LPWAN technologies, and IoT gateways. These topics gave me a broader perspective on how IoT systems operate beyond just the hardware.

As a first-year ECE student, this program strengthened my interest in embedded systems and automation. It improved my debugging skills, problem-solving ability, and confidence in integrating multiple sensors and communication protocols into a single project. More importantly, it showed me how IoT can be applied to solve practical problems in areas such as smart homes, agriculture, healthcare, and environmental monitoring.

Looking ahead, I want to continue building my skills in embedded systems, IoT, and industrial automation. I am especially interested in developing intelligent devices that combine sensors, wireless communication, and cloud technologies to make everyday systems more efficient, reliable, and safe.

If I were to build my next IoT project, I would create a Smart Hostel Safety and Energy Management System. The project would use PIR sensors to detect occupancy, MQ-2 gas sensors for smoke detection, DHT11 sensors to monitor temperature and humidity, and LDR sensors for automatic lighting control. An ESP32 would collect data from all the sensors and send it to a cloud dashboard using MQTT. Residents could receive alerts through a mobile application whenever smoke is detected or room conditions become unsafe. The system could also reduce unnecessary power consumption by automatically switching off lights in empty rooms while providing real-time monitoring of the hostel environment.

Overall, this IoT Summer School gave me practical exposure to embedded systems, sensor interfacing, wireless communication, cloud connectivity, and project documentation. It has strengthened the foundation I built during my first year of ECE and motivated me to continue learning and working on more advanced IoT and embedded systems projects in the future.
